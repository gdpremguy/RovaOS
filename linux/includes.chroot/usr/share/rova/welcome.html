WELCOME TO YOUR WORKSPACE

Your space. Your pace.

RovaOS is a portable Linux desktop that runs from a USB drive. It is designed to be fast, lightweight, and easy to use. You can take it with you and run it on any computer without leaving a trace.

A FEW GOOD PLACES TO START

Settle in.

01 / OPEN

Find your apps

Press Super + Space to search for an app. Open Firefox for the web, Files for your documents, or Terminal when you need it.

02 / CONNECT

Get online

Use the network menu in the system tray to select your Wi-Fi network and enter its password. Open Wi-Fi & networks from Control Center to edit saved connections or advanced settings.

For school or workplace networks, use the supplied authentication and CA certificate settings. Keep certificate verification enabled.

03 / PERSONALISE

Make it yours

Press Super + , for Rova Settings. Find display, sound, wallpaper and other workspace controls in one place.

TAKE YOUR WORK WITH YOU

Know what stays.

Temporary session

Try things freely.

Without confirmed USB saving, changes are lost when you shut down or restart. A file appearing on screen does not mean it has been saved to the USB.

Saving enabled

Pick up where you left off.

With persistence active, settings, personal files, browser data and saved Wi-Fi profiles can survive a clean restart. Control Center reports the actual session state.

Enable saving on your boot USB

  1. Open Control Center → USB saving setup.
  2. Review the detected boot USB and proposed save area, then confirm if everything is correct.
  3. Keep the USB connected while setup finishes, then restart.
  4. Check Control Center again to confirm that saving is active.

Setup uses spare space on the boot USB, up to 2 GiB by default, and refuses internal-drive targets. Some USB layouts or devices may not support this setup.

Files from the current temporary session are not migrated. Copy anything important elsewhere before restarting.

Your data deserves care.

Saved USB data is not encrypted (sorry!), including credentials. Anyone with the USB may be able to read it. Keep a separate backup and back up before reflashing, which can erase existing saves.

Keep the boot USB connected until the computer has fully powered off. Do not unplug it while the system is running or suspended. Internal drives are not installation targets; do not manually mount or modify them unless you intend to.

A SECOND COPY HELPS

Back up before you travel.

Open Control Center → Saving & backup → Export files & settings to export your home folder to another mounted filesystem. The export includes personal files, user settings and browser profiles; it excludes caches, Trash, installed system packages and system-wide Wi-Fi profiles. It is not a complete image of the save partition.

Close your apps before exporting. Keep the backup somewhere separate from the boot USB and treat it as private: archives are unencrypted and may contain passwords.

WHEN SOMETHING NEEDS A HAND

Find your footing.

No Wi-Fi networks?

Check airplane mode and your laptop's wireless switch or key. Run rova-network-report in Terminal to check drivers, radio blocks, NetworkManager and time sync without displaying saved Wi-Fi passwords.

Wireless support depends on your device, firmware and network configuration.

Display or desktop trouble?

Test display changes in Display & monitors; they revert after 15 seconds unless kept. Recover desktop can reset Niri settings after confirmation and preserves the previous settings in a dated folder. If the desktop cannot start, try desktop recovery in the boot menu. The safe-graphics console entry opens a temporary text console, not a graphical desktop.

Check the time

The default timezone is Australia/Adelaide. Online time synchronisation helps keep the clock accurate. A wrong clock can prevent secure websites from loading; reconnect and check time sync before changing security settings.
